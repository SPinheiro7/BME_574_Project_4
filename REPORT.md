1\. The track dataset we chose was blood. The image sizes are 28 x 28 and there are 3 channels. There are 8 classes: basophil 7.1%, eosinophil 18.2%, erythroblast 9.1%, immature granulocytes 16.9%, lymphocyte 7.1%, monocyte 8.3%, neutrophil 19.5%, and platelet 13.7%. There is a moderate imbalance in the dataset as they are not close to an even 12.5% across each class, where the majority-class baseline is neutrophil. In the sample grid, platelets are visibly smaller, while several white-cell classes have similar purple nuclei and differ more subtly in nuclear shape and surrounding cytoplasm.  
2\. The test accuracies for the CNN, matched-parameter dense network, and permuted CNN were 90.06%, 77.93%, and 82.20%, respectively. The CNN and dense network had 17,640 and 18,896 parameters, respectively. The CNN outperformed the dense network by 12.13 percentage points, suggesting that convolution provides an advantage even when the models have similar parameter counts. The permuted CNN performed 7.86 percentage points worse than the original CNN, indicating that preserving the spatial arrangement of pixels is important for classification. Unlike the dense network comparison, the permutation demonstrates that the CNN benefits from meaningful spatial relationships between pixels.  
3\. The transforms in the augmenter are horizontal flips, vertical flips, and rotations of 90°, 180°, and 270°, which were randomly applied to the training images, with some images left unchanged. They were included because blood cells in microscopic images do not have a fixed orientation, meaning that rotating or flipping them should not change their classification. One transformation we considered but rejected was hue and saturation jitter because color differences caused by staining can provide important information for distinguishing blood cell types. Therefore, modifying these color characteristics could alter biological features.  
4\. The unaugmented CNN achieved an accuracy of 90.06% on the ordinary set and 87.84% on the harder reoriented evaluation. In comparison, the augmented CNN achieved 89.54% and 89.62%, respectively. Therefore, augmentation decreased ordinary test accuracy by approximately 0.53% points but improved accuracy on the harder evaluation by 1.78% points. These results suggest that augmentation improved the model's ability to recognize blood cells despite changes in orientation, although it slightly reduced performance on the original test distribution. This also demonstrates that ordinary test accuracy does not necessarily measure robustness to image transformations, since a model can perform well on familiar orientations while being more sensitive to reoriented images.  
5\. The unaugmented model had higher validation accuracy (91.36% versus 90.30%) and was selected for the confusion matrix. Its test macro-F1 was 0.8842. Recalls were basophil 74.6%, eosinophil 98.4%, erythroblast 88.7%, immature granulocytes 84.1%, lymphocyte 88.1%, monocyte 72.2%, neutrophil 95.0%, and platelet 100.0%. The largest directional confusion was 66/284 monocytes classified as immature granulocytes. This confusion is reasonable because these two cell types can have similar nuclear shapes and cytoplasmic appearances, making them more difficult to distinguish, especially at a resolution of 28 × 28 pixels.  
6\. We would experiment with class-weighted cross-entropy loss, using weights calculated from the training-set class frequencies and selecting the best configuration based on validation macro-F1. We would choose this approach because monocytes and basophils had the lowest recalls at 72.2% and 74.6%, respectively. This suggests that the current model struggles to recognize these less-represented classes. By increasing their importance during training, I would expect their recall to improve, particularly by reducing the number of monocytes misclassified as immature granulocytes. However, this could slightly reduce performance for other classes, so we would evaluate changes in per-class recall and macro-F1 to determine whether the experiment was successful.
